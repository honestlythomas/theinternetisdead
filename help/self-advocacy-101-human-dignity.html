Defining Terms of Human Dignity

A working vocabulary for describing rights, relationships, support, control, and the conditions people need to participate in their own lives.

Thomas Harrison / Honestly Thomas Self-Advocacy 101 · 2026
Agency
Your ability to make choices and act on them.
Autonomy
Control over your own body, life, and decisions.
Independence
Living without unnecessary control or forced reliance.
Interdependence
People supporting each other without taking away autonomy.
Support
Help that increases your ability to live your own life.
Protection
Preventing harm without becoming controlling.
Safety
Freedom from danger, coercion, exploitation, and abandonment.
Dignity
Being treated as a person of equal human worth.
Sovereignty
Rightful authority over yourself.
Consent
Freely agreeing with adequate information and a real choice.
Capacity
Your present ability to understand and make a particular decision.
Accommodation
Changing a process so disability does not block access.
Accessibility
Making participation genuinely possible.
Advocacy
Communicating and defending someone’s needs, choices, and rights.
Self-Advocacy
Representing your own needs and lived reality.
Supported Decision-Making
Receiving help with decisions without surrendering control.
Continuity
Support that does not disappear between appointments or referrals.
Accountability
Responsibility for actions, failures, and outcomes.
Transparency
Clearly explaining decisions, processes, and limitations.
Validation
Recognizing someone’s experience as real.
Trust
Confidence built through honesty and consistent action.
Boundaries
Limits that protect a person’s time, body, energy, and identity.
Neglect
Failing to provide necessary care or protection.
Abandonment
Withdrawing support while significant needs remain unresolved.
Dependency
Relying on something or someone to meet essential needs.
Empowerment
Increasing someone’s choices, confidence, and control.
Community
Reciprocal human connection and belonging.
Inclusion
Being welcomed as a participant, not merely managed as a problem.
Humanity
The whole person beyond diagnoses, records, risks, and services.

Language does not create human dignity. It gives people tools to identify when dignity is being supported, restricted, or denied.
